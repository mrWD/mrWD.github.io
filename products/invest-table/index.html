InvestTable

A compound-growth table, ported from the spreadsheet it replaces. You type what you pay in every month and what it earns; it gives you the balance for every month, for up to thirty years.

What it looks like

What it does

  • 📅

    Every month, not just the total

    One row per month for up to thirty years: what the balance is after that month's deposit, and what it earned. Grouped into years you can collapse, so year nine is a tap away rather than a scroll.

  • 🔁

    A rate in whatever unit you have it

    Per month, per quarter or per year — a fund's annual figure, a bond's quarterly coupon. Switching the unit only relabels the rate; the projection does not move. The conversion compounds: 20% a year is 1.531% a month, not 1.667%.

  • 💵

    Dividends on what you hold

    An annual dividend rate on the previous year's closing balance, spread across twelve months — the figure the position would pay you monthly without selling any of it.

  • 📈

    A chart with a log scale

    Balance against money paid in. Compounding spends most of the horizon looking flat and then leaves the frame; on a log scale a constant rate is a straight line, so the early years become readable.

  • 🌍

    Twenty languages, properly

    Not just translated strings: numbers, percentages and plurals follow the language. German reads “25 Mio.”, Japanese “2500万”, Hindi groups by lakh, and Arabic mirrors the whole layout — while the chart stays left to right, as it should.

  • 🔒

    Nothing leaves the device

    No account, no server, no analytics. Your figures live in the browser's own storage. The one network request is a daily exchange-rate table, and the app is fully usable when it fails.

Where the numbers come from

It began as a Google Sheet called “Revenue Chart”, and the model is a port rather than a rewrite. Each month the balance earns the rate and takes another deposit; the sheet rounds every cell to cents and compounds the rounded figure, so its column drifts a cent above exact arithmetic — and so does this one, on purpose. A table that is “more correct” than the one it replaces is a table nobody can check.

That fidelity is tested rather than asserted: a script asserts 107 values, 72 of them read straight off the original spreadsheet, and it runs on every change to the model.

What it isn't

A projection, not a forecast. It assumes the return you type in arrives every month without fail and is reinvested in full, and it counts no fees, taxes, spreads or inflation. Real returns vary and can be negative. Nothing here is investment advice, and the app says so on its own front page.

It also connects to nothing: no broker, no portfolio, no prices. You type the numbers; it does the arithmetic and shows its work.
