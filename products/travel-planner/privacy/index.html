Travel Planner — privacy policy

Travel Planner has no account and no server of its own. Your trips and saved places stay on your device. To draw maps and find places, it asks public services directly from your device — never through us.

What this covers

This policy is about the Travel Planner app — the iOS build distributed through TestFlight, the Android build and the web version. The site you are reading it on is covered separately by Legal & privacy.

Travel Planner is an independent project. There is no company backend, no sign-up and no user account. “We” below means the developer of the app.

What we store

Nothing. Everything the app keeps lives on your device: your trips and their plans, the places you saved, the posts read from a data export you imported (their links, captions, authors and hashtags), your settings, and a cache of place lookups so they are not repeated. In the installed app that is the app’s own private storage; on the web it is your browser’s storage.

A data export from Instagram, TikTok or YouTube is read on the device and never uploaded. Only your saved posts are taken from it; nothing else in it is kept.

If you add an Anthropic API key, it is kept apart from everything else: in the iOS Keychain or the Android Keystore (on the web, in your browser’s storage).

What leaves your device

To do its job the app asks public services for map and place data. Each request goes from your device straight to the service — which therefore sees your IP address — and that service’s own privacy policy applies to it:

  • OpenStreetMap map tiles (and CARTO’s, if OpenStreetMap’s fail): the area of the map you look at.
  • Nominatim, Photon and Overpass (OpenStreetMap data): the destination you type, and place names from your imported posts’ captions, to find where they are.
  • Wikipedia and Wikidata: a location, to find the sights and towns around it.
  • Instagram: for an older export that lists only links, the address of each saved post’s public embed page, to read its caption. No login is used and nothing is posted. (The web version asks through its own server, because browsers may not call Instagram directly; that server passes the request on and keeps nothing.) TikTok and YouTube: a saved video’s address, for its title.
  • Anthropic — only if you added your own key: the names and categories of a plan’s places, your interests and the city, to write the day summaries and tips. The request is made with your key, under your agreement with Anthropic.

Look up post captions online, on the Import screen, turns the import’s online lookups off; the import then works offline. Links you tap — directions, ticket searches, a post, a Wikipedia article — open in your maps app or browser.

Analytics

None, in the installed app and on the web alike. No page views, no events, no identifiers, and no crash reporting of our own.

Deleting your data

Settings → Delete all data removes every trip, saved place, imported post and the API key from the device; the key also has a delete button of its own. Deleting the app removes everything.

Beta testing

While the app is distributed through TestFlight, Apple collects the installation and crash data described in Apple’s TestFlight privacy notice. That is Apple’s collection under Apple’s terms. It reaches us only as anonymous crash reports and the feedback you choose to send, and never includes your trips, your saved places or anything you imported.

Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top of the page changes with it, and anything newly sent anywhere is described here before it ships.
