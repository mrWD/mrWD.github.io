IT Skills — delete your account

How to delete your account in IT Skills: Senior Path, the app by Viktor Lavrov on the App Store and Google Play, together with everything our server keeps for it. You can do it in the app, or by e-mail without it.

In the app

  1. Open Profile

    If the app opens on its account screen, choose Sign in, enter the account’s e-mail and password, tap Sign in, then open Profile.

    Otherwise, tap the Profile tab.

  2. Find the Account card

    It is the 👤 Account card, under the language. If it says Signed in as and the address of the account you want to delete, go on to step 3.

    Signed in to another account? Tap Sign out. If some changes could not be sent yet, the app asks first, and Sign out anyway loses them. The account screen opens: choose Sign in, enter the account’s e-mail and password, and tap Sign in.

    Not signed in? The card says you are using the app without an account. Tap Sign in or sign up, choose Sign in, enter the account’s e-mail and password, and tap Sign in.

  3. Tap Delete account

    At the bottom of the Account card, beside Sign out.

  4. Confirm with Delete

    The app asks Delete the account? Tap Delete. The account is deleted at once and cannot be brought back; the app then shows its account screen again.

The labels above are the app’s English ones; in another language the buttons are in the same places. Signing in on a device that already has progress adds that progress to the account, so it is deleted with it. To keep it, first tap Profile → Backup → Copy the backup. After the deletion, choose Continue without an account, answer the starting questions, then Profile → Backup → paste it → Restore from the backup → Restore.

Forgot the password? On the account screen choose Sign in, then Forgot your password?: the app e-mails a 6-digit code to the account’s address. Or write to us, as below. Deleting the app or signing out does not delete the account: it stays on the server until you delete it.

Without the app

Write to lvigtor@gmail.com from the e-mail address of the account, with the subject “Delete my IT Skills account”. Before we delete anything or send a copy, we write to that address to confirm. If you no longer have that mailbox but know the password, delete the account in the app as above; without either, we cannot tell that the account is yours. Once you confirm, we delete the account with everything listed below and reply when it is done — within one month at the latest.

The same way you can ask for a copy of everything our server keeps for your account.

E-mail a deletion request

What is deleted

Everything our server keeps for the account, from its database, at once:

  • the e-mail address, the password hash and any password-reset code;
  • the records of your devices (id, model, platform, dates) and every sign-in;
  • all your progress, with its whole history: your name, goal and GitHub username, skill levels, lessons and tests, enrolled courses, the files and code of your course projects with their earlier versions, links to published projects, certificates, interview results, XP, streak and the app’s language.

Every device is signed out of the account. The device you delete it from in the app keeps no progress, and the saved GitHub token is removed from it.

What is kept, and for how long

  • Backups — Fly.io, which hosts our server, copies the database disk every day and keeps each copy for five days. Your account is gone from them within about a week.
  • The server log — lines saying that the account was created, signed in, changed or reset its password or was deleted, with the time and the random ids of the account and its devices. No e-mail address, no password, no progress. Fly.io keeps the log for 7 days.
  • Password-reset e-mails — if we ever sent you a code, a copy of that e-mail (your address and the code, which stopped working after 15 minutes) stays in the Gmail mailbox it was sent from. We delete these copies within 30 days, and at once when we delete an account at your request. If you asked for the deletion by e-mail, the messages we exchanged about it stay there, as the record that it was done.
  • The server’s memory — if you recently signed in or asked for a reset code, your e-mail and IP address may still be counted there against password guessing. They are never written anywhere, and are dropped some time after the count has filled back up (an hour at most for an IP address, three hours for an e-mail address), when the server next checks that limit or restarts for an update.
  • Your other devices — each device that was signed in to the account (every device, if we deleted it at your e-mailed request) is signed out the next time it connects, but keeps its copy of the progress, without an account, until the app is deleted there. If you sign in on that device again, to any account, that copy joins the account you sign in to.
  • What is yours elsewhere — repositories you published stay in your GitHub account; delete them on GitHub. Backups you copied stay wherever you pasted them, and your phone’s own backups follow their own schedule.

Only the progress?

Profile → Reset progress, while you are signed in, clears your progress on every device and keeps the account. The server keeps the earlier history of your progress until the account is deleted. To remove that too, delete the account.
