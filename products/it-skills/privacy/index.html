IT Skills — privacy policy

IT Skills works without an account, and then your progress stays on your device. An account is optional: it keeps your progress on the IT Skills server, hosted in Frankfurt, so it syncs across your devices. The courses come from the same server either way. No analytics, no advertising, no tracking.

What this covers

This policy is about the IT Skills app, listed on the App Store and Google Play as “IT Skills: Senior Path” — the iOS build distributed through TestFlight and the Android build distributed through Google Play — and the IT Skills server behind it, itskills-api.fly.dev. The site you are reading it on is covered separately by Legal & privacy.

IT Skills is an independent project by Viktor Lavrov, who decides what happens to the data described here — the controller, in the words of the GDPR. “We” below means him. Contact: lvigtor@gmail.com.

Without an account

The app keeps everything in its own storage on your device: the name you typed in, your goal, your self-assessment and skill levels, lesson and test results, the files of your course projects, certificates and interview scores. None of it is sent to us: without an account we have no copy.

The app still talks to our server to get the courses: the course list, skills and goals when it starts, when you switch its language, and at most every ten minutes when you come back to it; and a course when you enrol in it or open it. These requests say which language the app is in, which course is wanted and whether in JavaScript or Python, and which version of it the device already has. They carry no account, no device identifier and nothing you entered. Like any website, our server and its host see the IP address a request comes from; our server does not record these requests or that address — only a request that fails with a server error is logged, with the path it asked for. That is why the first start needs the internet; a course you have downloaded then works offline.

With an account

You create an account in the app with an e-mail address and a password. It lives on our server, itskills-api.fly.dev, hosted by Fly.io in Frankfurt, Germany, which keeps:

  • Your account — your e-mail address; your password, stored only as a salted scrypt hash, never as the password itself; and when you confirmed the address with a password-reset code, if you ever did. We do not check that an address is yours when you sign up.
  • Your devices — a record for every sign-in on a device: a random id, its model, such as “iPhone 15 Pro” or “Pixel 8” (never the name you gave your phone), whether it runs iOS or Android, when it signed in, when it last connected and when it was signed out. A sign-in is a random token of which the server keeps only a hash; one unused for 180 days stops working. Signing out ends the sign-in, but the device’s record stays with the account.
  • Your progress — the name you typed in (or the default, such as “Student”); your goal: the role, level and track you are aiming for; your GitHub username, if you entered one; your self-assessment, your skill levels and the skills marked to work on; lessons with their scores, and tests passed or failed with their scores; the courses you enrolled in, their order, depth and code language; the files of your course projects, with the code you wrote in them; the names and links of projects you published to GitHub, or whose publishing the app simulated; your certificates; your mock interview results — the track, the date, the score, the skills to work on and the skill levels your strongest answers earned, never the answers themselves; XP, your streak and the app’s language.

The server keeps your progress as a history of changes — each with its time on your device, the time it arrived and the device it came from — plus the current state built from them. That is how a device that was offline catches up. The history includes earlier versions of your project files: the app saves them as you type and sends the latest a couple of seconds after you stop, and once a minute while you keep typing.

When you create an account, sign in, or set a new password with a reset code, the progress already on the device joins the account; the sign-up and sign-in screens say so. A backup you restore while signed in replaces your account’s progress.

The code you type in a lesson’s exercises is not kept anywhere — only the files of course projects are — and your answers in a mock interview never leave the device. Course downloads are the same for everyone and are not linked to your account.

Password reset

If you forget your password, Forgot your password? on the sign-in screen sends a 6-digit code to the account’s e-mail address. The server keeps only a hash of the code, with its times and the tries spent on it. The code works once, for 15 minutes, and takes at most 5 tries; an account takes at most 10 wrong codes a day. Setting a new password with it signs every other device out of the account.

The e-mail is sent from our Gmail account, lvigtor@gmail.com, through Google’s mail servers. It holds your address and the code, in Russian if the app is in Russian and in English otherwise. Gmail keeps a copy of every message sent from that mailbox, and a message that cannot be delivered comes back to it. We delete these copies within 30 days, and at once when we delete an account at your request; the code in them stops working 15 minutes after it is sent. This is the only e-mail the server sends: no newsletters, no marketing.

Other services

Apart from our server, the app talks to others only when you ask it to. When you press Publish to GitHub on a finished course project and have saved a token, the app asks GitHub for your username and sends that project’s files and the repository name to api.github.com, authenticated with your token, to create a public repository in your account. That request goes from your device straight to GitHub — never through us — and GitHub’s own privacy policy applies to what it receives. Without a token, publishing is simulated on the device and nothing is sent to GitHub. If you are signed in, the link to the repository — published or simulated — becomes part of your progress, and so does the GitHub username you entered; the token never does.

Your code runs on the device, Python included. Interview answers are scored on the device by matching key terms, not by an online AI service. A lesson you listen to is read aloud by your device’s speech engine; some of its voices may work online, under the terms of the engine’s maker. Links you tap open in your browser, and sharing a certificate or copying a backup happen only when you choose to.

On your device

Your progress and the courses you downloaded live in the app’s own private storage. The sign-in to your account and the GitHub personal access token, if you give one, are kept apart from everything else, in the iOS Keychain or the Android Keystore. They work only on this device: a backup does not bring them to another one, and the token is never sent to our server.

Signing out removes your progress and the token from the device; the progress stays in the account. If some changes could not be sent yet, the app asks first, and Sign out anyway loses them. Downloaded courses stay on the device.

Deleting the app deletes its storage, with two exceptions that belong to your phone, not to the app: your phone’s own backup to iCloud or Google may include the app’s storage and bring it back when you reinstall; and on iOS the Keychain can keep the sign-in and the token after the app is deleted, so a reinstalled app may start signed in.

In the browser, at mrwd.github.io/it-skills, the same things live in the browser’s storage for mrwd.github.io: your progress and the sign-in in its local storage, the downloaded courses in its database (IndexedDB), and a copy of the app’s own files so that it opens offline. A GitHub token is kept only in memory and is gone when the tab is closed. Clearing the site’s data in the browser deletes all of it.

Who else handles it

Fly.io, Inc. hosts the server and its database in Frankfurt, and keeps the database’s daily backups, as our processor. Google provides the Gmail account the reset e-mail is sent from; it receives your address and the code and keeps them under Google’s own terms and privacy policy, in Google’s data centres, which may be outside the EU. Fly.io, Inc. and Google LLC are US companies, and both take part in the EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework.

Connections from the app are encrypted (HTTPS) as far as Fly.io’s network, whose nearest point may be outside the EU; from there they reach our server inside Fly.io’s private network. The database can be reached only from that private network, and its disk is encrypted. This is not end-to-end encryption: our server reads what the app sends it, and your e-mail address, name and progress are stored as they are, protected by the disk’s encryption and by who can reach the server. The reset e-mail leaves our server for Google encrypted (TLS).

Like any server, ours sees the IP address each request comes from. It uses it for one thing: to limit how often one address can create an account, sign in, or ask for or enter a reset code. For the same reason it counts sign-ins and reset codes per e-mail address, and requests per account. These counts are kept only in the server’s memory (the wrong reset codes of an account are counted with the code, above), never written to its database or its log. An address is dropped some time after its count has filled back up — an hour at most for an IP address, three hours for an e-mail address — when the server next checks that limit or restarts for an update; until then it can stay in memory for days.

Nobody else gets your data. We do not sell it, share it, or use it for advertising or tracking.

Server log

The server writes a line to its log when an account is created, a device signs in, a password is changed or reset, or an account is deleted. The line holds the time and the random ids of the account and, where there is one, the device — no e-mail address, no password, no IP address and no progress. A sent reset e-mail is logged without the address; a failed one with the mail server’s error, from which the address is cut out. A request turned away for coming too often is logged with the limit it hit, not with who sent it. An error is logged with the kind of request that failed and where the server broke, not with what the request carried. Fly.io keeps the log for 7 days.

How long it is kept

  • Account, devices and progress, with the whole history of changes — until you delete the account. An account is not deleted for being unused, and Reset progress does not delete the history.
  • Sign-ins — until you sign out, until a password change signs the device out, or until 180 days after their last use. The token’s record is then deleted within about a week; the device’s record stays with the account.
  • A reset code — works for 15 minutes. Its record (the hash, times and tries) stays until you ask for a new code, change the password in the app or delete the account.
  • Copies of reset e-mails — in our Gmail mailbox; we delete them within 30 days, and at once when we delete an account at your request.
  • IP and e-mail addresses counted against guessing — in memory only, as above.
  • The server log — for the 7 days Fly.io keeps it.
  • Backups — Fly.io copies the database disk every day and keeps each copy for five days, encrypted like the disk itself. What you delete is gone from them within about a week.

Your control and your rights

In the app, Profile → Account → Delete account deletes your account at once, with everything the server keeps for it: the e-mail, the password hash, your devices and sign-ins, reset codes, and all your progress with its history. Step by step, and how to ask us by e-mail instead: Delete your account.

Every device is signed out. The device you delete it from keeps no progress. Any other device that was signed in to the account keeps its copy of the progress, now without an account, until the app is deleted there (Reset progress does not remove that copy); if you sign in on that device again, to any account, the copy joins the account you sign in to.

Sign out removes your progress from the device, not from the account. Profile → Reset progress, while you are signed in, clears your progress on every device of the account, but the server keeps the earlier history until you delete the account.

You can change your name, goal and GitHub username in the app. The app cannot change the e-mail address: write to us, confirm the change from the old and the new address, and we change it. Profile → Copy the backup gives you your current progress as text; for everything the server keeps about you, history included, write to us.

Under the GDPR you can ask us for a copy of your data, to correct it, to delete it, to take it elsewhere, or to restrict or object to how we use it — write to lvigtor@gmail.com. If the request is about your account, we first write to the account’s address and act once you confirm from it. We answer within one month. We keep account data to give you what you signed up for (the contract, GDPR art. 6(1)(b)); we count IP and e-mail addresses against password guessing and keep the server log to keep accounts and the server safe (legitimate interest, art. 6(1)(f)). You can also complain to a data protection authority, for example the one where you live — the EU authorities.

Copying a backup

Profile → Copy the backup puts your progress on the clipboard as text, at your request, so you can keep it wherever you like and restore it on another device. It goes wherever you paste it, not to us — unless you restore it while signed in: then it becomes your account’s progress. The GitHub token is not part of it.

Permissions

The app asks for no permissions: no camera, microphone, photos, contacts, location or notifications.

Analytics

None. No page views, no analytics events, no advertising identifiers and no crash reporting of our own: the app contains no analytics, advertising or crash-reporting code. What the server keeps is described above and is used only to run your account.

Beta testing

While the app is distributed through TestFlight, Apple collects the installation, usage and crash data described in Apple’s TestFlight privacy notice. That is Apple’s collection under Apple’s terms. It reaches us as install and session counts, crash reports and the feedback you choose to send, and never includes your progress.

On Android the app is distributed through open testing on Google Play, and Google collects the installation and crash data described in Google’s privacy policy. That, too, is Google’s collection under Google’s terms. It reaches us as install counts, crash reports and the feedback you choose to send, and never includes your progress.

Children

The app is not directed at children. Without an account your progress never leaves your device, at any age. Please don’t create an account if you are under 16, unless where you live you may agree to this yourself at a younger age. The app does not ask your age; if a child has created an account, write to us and we will delete it.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the date at the top of the page changes with it, and anything new the app or the server keeps or sends anywhere is described here before it ships. Questions and requests: Viktor Lavrov, lvigtor@gmail.com.
