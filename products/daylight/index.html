Daylight Health Coach

A personal health and training advisor, not another dashboard of charts. It collects the data for you and shows only what matters today: whether to train hard, go easy or rest, and why. Everything is worked out on your phone and watch.

  • Free
  • No account
  • No servers at all
  • Apple Watch
  • RU · EN · IT · DE

Where it stands

Daylight is in testing. It runs on an iPhone and an Apple Watch, and the first TestFlight build is being processed by Apple. There are no screenshots on this page yet.

Everything is calculated on the device by ordinary rules. The language model only rephrases an already-checked answer.

What it does

  • 🌅

    Three decisions, not a dashboard

    The Today screen gives three or four cards, each with a plain answer and a “Why?”: the numbers behind it, what the app took into account and how sure it is. Morning mode shows the three things for today, evening mode the three from today.

  • 📏

    Your own baseline

    Every figure is compared with your usual range over four weeks — the median and the middle half — not with a universal norm. Until there is a week of data the app says it is still learning.

  • 🔋

    Recovery and load

    Sleep, HRV, resting heart rate, breathing, temperature, yesterday’s load and how you feel become a recovery status. Training load joins workouts, strength sets and steps into one number and compares it with your own month.

  • 🏋️

    A strength log you can type

    “Bench 60x8, 65x6, 70x5” is parsed without any model: sets, volume, an estimated one-rep max, repeats at the same weight, personal records and plateaus.

  • 🏃

    Every workout, named

    Apple Health knows about eighty kinds of workout, from volleyball to dives. The journal shows each one with its own icon, calories, distance, heart rate, depth and where it came from — Apple Watch, iPhone or another app.

  • 🛡️

    Two safety engines

    One watches training: a jump in weight or volume, three hard days in a row, too little recovery. One watches health: blood pressure, resting heart rate, SpO₂, temperature, symptoms. They only ever say “discuss it with a doctor”, never “you have…”.

  • 🌙

    Nights, breathing, daylight

    Night-time trips to the toilet (with an evening-water hint), breathing disturbances in sleep, a one-week blood-pressure protocol, minutes in daylight, and how insomnia nights add up.

  • 🍷

    What alcohol and coffee do to you

    One tap in the morning, or Health entries. The app shows how alcohol changes your HRV and resting heart rate, and whether late coffee shortens your sleep.

  • 🎧

    Hearing and sitting

    Weekly headphone sound dose by the WHO yardstick, with warnings at 80 and 100 per cent. A nudge to stand up when the day adds up to more than ten hours of sitting, silenced for illness, heat, pain or when you switch it off.

  • ⌚

    On the Apple Watch too

    Recovery status, the top three tips with “Why?”, a one-tap feeling and symptom check-in, and a quick set log with a local weight-jump check. It all travels to the iPhone over WatchConnectivity.

How an answer is made

  1. Collect

    Apple Health (sleep with stages, heart rate, HRV, breathing, SpO₂, temperature, weight, blood pressure, steps, workouts, water, daylight, headphone volume, Stand ring) plus your own log and check-ins.

  2. Measure

    Metrics, your four-week baseline, progress, recovery and training load. A day without observations is unknown, not “rest”, so a new user is never told they are overtrained.

  3. Check safety

    The two safety engines can remove “push harder”, cap the recovery status and block any recommendation of a working weight. Red symptoms, such as chest discomfort or shortness of breath, stop the training advice altogether.

  4. Say it

    Apple’s on-device model rewrites the checked text in plain words. If it ever names a weight or an illness, its answer is thrown away and the rule-based wording is shown instead.

Where the data lives

On your iPhone and your Apple Watch. There is no account, no server and no sync service of mine: the app contains no networking code at all. Health data is read from Apple Health and is never written back; your strength log, check-ins and blood-pressure readings are small files in the app’s own storage. The watch and the phone talk to each other directly.

The full detail is in the privacy policy. Data confidence is shown on every conclusion: Apple Watch is high, third-party apps medium, typed-in values and recognised text low.

Languages

Russian, English, Italian and German, on the phone and on the watch. The language follows the device; anything else falls back to English.

What it isn’t

Not a medical device, and not a doctor. It does not diagnose, treat or prevent anything, and it never tells you what to lift: it will say a jump looks too big and ask you to check the entry, not what the next weight should be. Numbers from a watch are estimates, and a short or noisy history can mislead.

Chest pain, difficulty breathing, fainting, or anything that frightens you is for emergency services, not an app. Please read the terms of use and health notice.
