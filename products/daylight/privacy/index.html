Daylight — privacy policy

Daylight has no servers and no accounts. Your health data stays on your iPhone and Apple Watch, and the app contains no code that talks to the internet.

What this covers

This policy is about the Daylight Health Coach app for iPhone and its Apple Watch companion, distributed through TestFlight and, later, the App Store. The site you are reading it on is covered separately by Legal & privacy.

Daylight is an independent project. There is no company backend, no sign-up and no user account. “We” below means the developer of the app.

What the app reads from Apple Health

Only after you allow it, and only the types you allow, in the system Health permission sheet: sleep and its stages, resting and walking heart rate, heart-rate variability, respiratory rate, blood oxygen, body and wrist temperature, weight, blood pressure, steps, distance, active energy, VO₂ max, workouts with their heart rate and depth, water, time in daylight, headphone sound levels, the Stand ring, mindful sessions, sleep-breathing events, Apple Watch high-heart-rate notifications, the emotions and moods you log in Health, and the GAD-7 and PHQ-9 questionnaires you fill in there. Around an anxiety episode you mark, it also reads your heart rate, steps and the nearest HRV measurement.

The app never writes anything to Apple Health. You can withdraw any permission at any time in Health → your profile → Apps → Daylight, or in Settings → Health → Data Access & Devices.

What the app stores

On your device only, in the app’s own storage: the strength sessions you type in, workouts you log by hand, your daily check-ins (how you feel, symptoms, nights you woke up or slept badly, alcohol and caffeine marks), the panic attacks and anxiety episodes you mark and what you answered about high-heart-rate notifications, blood-pressure readings you enter, the state of the one-week blood-pressure protocol, and a few settings, such as reminder times and the switch that silences “move more” advice. iOS keeps the files protected until you first unlock the device after a restart. The Apple Watch keeps the latest summary the phone sent it.

Like any app’s files, they can be part of the encrypted device backups you make yourself, to iCloud or to a computer. The app does not use iCloud on its own, and none of it ever reaches me.

What leaves your device

Nothing. The app has no networking code: it does not contact any server of mine, of an analytics company or of anyone else. There is no advertising and no tracking, and the app’s privacy manifest says so.

Three things do move, none of them over the internet to me. The iPhone and the Apple Watch exchange the summary and your check-ins directly through Apple’s WatchConnectivity. Reminders are local notifications scheduled on the phone, not push messages. And the language model is Apple’s on-device model, used through the Foundation Models framework: it rephrases the checked wording and, if you ask, reads a workout you typed in free form, on the device. Whether Apple Intelligence is available, and how Apple handles it, is governed by Apple’s own terms.

Analytics and crash reports

None of my own. While the app is on TestFlight, Apple collects installation and crash data as described in Apple’s TestFlight privacy notice; that is Apple’s collection under Apple’s terms. It reaches me only as anonymous crash reports and the feedback you choose to send, and never includes your health data.

Deleting your data

Deleting the app removes everything it stored on the phone and, with the watch app, on the watch. Your Apple Health data belongs to Health and is not touched. Because I hold no copy, there is nothing for me to export or erase on request.

Your rights in the EU and elsewhere

Health data, including mental health, is a special category under the GDPR. Daylight processes it only on your device, under your control and for your own use, and I do not receive it, so I am not a controller of it. If you nonetheless email me, I use the message only to answer it and delete it afterwards on request. You can complain to your national data-protection authority — in Italy the Garante, in Germany the authority of your federal state.

Children

The app is meant for adults. It is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone, of any age.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top of the page changes with it. Since the app collects nothing, any change would be about something newly added — and it would be described here before it shipped.
